The story behind FM Radio

It started over a beer in early 2025...

While brainstorming with a friend, we started discussing FM radio as a musical effect. At the time, I was deep into Max/MSP, gen~, and Eurorack, and was looking for new DSP projects to explore.

The next morning, I started experimenting with frequency modulation and demodulation of audio signals. After a couple of days, I had built the first version of a system for simulating FM radio transmission and reception entirely at audio rate in Max.

Later that year, while browsing the Max Discord, I happened to come across an announcement for PdMaxCon25~, a conference on Max and Pure Data with an open call for submissions. I thought it would be a good opportunity to clean up my FM radio patches and ideas and turn them into a short paper. I submitted it and was very happy to find out that it was accepted.

In September, I travelled to Chicago and the University of Illinois, where I spent a few days with fellow Max, Pd, and music-tech enthusiasts and presented the work in one of the paper sessions. If you're interested in experimenting with FM radio in Max, the DSP implementation and Max abstractions from the project are available for free on my GitHub.

From early on, I also had the idea of turning the DSP system into a more user-friendly Max for Live device. That eventually became FM Radio: a device that places up to eight virtual stations across an FM band and lets you continuously tune between them. I wanted to keep it flexible and simple to use, so audio from any track in Ableton Live can be routed to any of the eight stations. I also added some simple controls for the FM parameters and a three-band EQ, allowing you to shape the sound from clean reception to heavily distorted and noisy radio signals.

The device is now available on Gumroad. I hope you enjoy it!

/ Henrik

SSLL DSP is live

The site is up: a home for the Max for Live devices, the Max patches behind them, and the writing that comes out of building them.

This site brings together work that has been running alongside my research — Max for Live devices, the Max patches they grew out of, and the tutorials and papers that came with them.

SSLL DSP is an independent project, so it moves at the pace of curiosity rather than deadlines. New work appears under Devices as it is finished and documented, and the writing follows when there is something worth saying about how it works.

First up is FM Radio, which puts eight stereo sources on a shared FM band and lets you tune between them.
